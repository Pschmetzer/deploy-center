Count your builds before you cut them

Do not start with the setting. Start with the count: builds are pushes times connected projects, and a few sources usually make most of them. Find those, cut them in order, and measure again, with your own numbers.

Last updated 21 November 2026 · by Patrick Schmetzer, author of Deploy Center

Deutsche Fassung

The formula

builds per day = pushes x projects connected to the repository

The numbers below are made up to show the arithmetic, not measurements. If a repository with 3 connected projects gets 10 pushes a day, that is 30 builds. Two of those 10 pushes come from a bot, and it is still 6 of the 30 builds. If only one project changed in each push, 2 of the 3 builds were for nothing.

Find where yours come from

deploys history web 20     # the last 20 deploys of one project
deploys costs 14           # Vercel: charges per day for 14 days
deploys triggers           # builds nobody started from here, last 24 hours
deploys simulate --days 30 # an estimate against one deploy a day

Your host's usage page is the reference for what it billed. The commands help you see which projects and which days stand out. Every estimate from Deploy Center is labelled as one.

Cut in this order

  1. Bots and agents. Their branches are many and nobody opens the previews: previews are for people.
  2. Projects a commit did not touch. See monorepo ignore rules.
  3. Production pushes. Turn auto-deploy off and deploy what changed once, when you decide.
  4. Build time. Only now is a faster build worth the effort, because every earlier step removes whole builds.

Measure again

After a week, run deploys ledger for an estimate of what you saved, and compare the host's usage page with the week before. If a build still appears that you did not expect, deploys explain <project> and deploys triggers tell you what started it.

What you give up

Fewer builds mean fewer previews and a fix that is not live until you deploy it. The count tells you which of those trades is cheap: a preview nobody opens costs build time and gives nothing back. See the build-minutes guide for the full list.

Questions

Why count before changing settings?

Because a few sources usually make most of the builds, and cutting the wrong one saves little. The count points at the right one.

Are the numbers in the example real?

No. They are made up to show the formula. Use your own host's usage page for real numbers.

Does Deploy Center measure my bill?

It estimates from your deploy history and, for Vercel, reads charges per day. Your host's invoice is the source of truth.

What if I do not use Vercel?

The formula and the order are the same. Use your host's usage page and deploys history for the project.
